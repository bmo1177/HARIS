## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment: category-interchangeable.** Strip the wordmark and this is a language tutor, a compliance quiz, or a support dashboard. The composition is the default answer: a 640px centred column, a sticky bar with five links, a stack of bordered cards, one cyan accent, mono micro-labels, restrained radii. Nothing in the structure says *investigate a message*. The message is a textarea that disappears; the verdict arrives as a score with no evidence attached. The one authored moment is the risk readout — and it is also where the design overclaims most (see Priority 2). The product's own thesis is "the clue-reveal structure is the product, not a flourish"; the result screen is a pronouncement, not a structure to read.

**Deterministic scan:** `impeccable detect --json src` — exit 0, **0 findings across 75 files**. This is not a silent no-op: a synthetic control containing a purple gradient returned exit 2 with `ai-color-palette` at line 3. The rules are style-oriented, so a clean scan here mostly says the source contains none of the classic tells. It says nothing about the semantic problems that matter: the detector cannot see that the language tabs are dead, that the score is false precision, or that the message is missing.

**Browser overlay:** injection succeeded on all five routes (`impeccableDetect` and `impeccableScan` became functions; live server on 8400, stopped afterwards). It found **4 warnings on `/sandbox`** — three `line-length` (~91, ~87, ~107 chars/line on the top callout, the Red Team objective, and the repeated bottom safety note) and one `layout-transition` reported on `body` on every route. The `layout-transition` is a **false positive**: computed `body` transition is `all 0s`; the detector is attributing Sonner's `transition: … height 0.4s` to `body`. The three line-length findings are real, and the 107-character one is a 12px safety note, which is the worst place for a long measure.

**Runtime evidence:** zero horizontal overflow on `/` and `/scenarios` at 1440×900 and 390×844, in both LTR and RTL — the bilingual layout is genuinely solid. Zero console errors and zero failed requests on all five routes. All eight tab stops on `/` have a visible 2px focus ring. Body text renders at 16px/24px in IBM Plex Sans.

## Overall Impression

The engineering underneath this is better than the design on top of it. Focus rings work, RTL does not overflow at 320px, errors are localized and specific, and the wait state has real craft. But the app's single most important screen — the result — throws away the thing being analysed, presents a four-value categorical judgement as an 84% instrument reading, and offers a language switch that does not switch. For a product whose stated purpose is teaching people to read evidence, that is the whole ballgame.

The biggest opportunity is not cosmetic. It is to make the message the object of the interface: the specimen the verdict is about, with the clues attached to it. That single structural move would make this look like nothing else in its category, because everything else in its category hides the evidence too.

## What's Working

1. **The analysis wait state.** Staged status copy, an elapsed counter, a real cancel, and a promise ("usually 15–30 seconds") that now matches the model. It is the one place where the product behaves like something with a point of view, and it buys patience the result then spends badly.
2. **Error handling and copy.** Typed errors become human sentences in both languages; a rate limit becomes "come back in N seconds" with a disabled button and a countdown. The 404 page is better written than most production apps' home pages.
3. **The bilingual foundation.** `lang`/`dir` are correct, Plex Sans Arabic is a first-class face rather than a fallback, nothing overflows at 320px in either direction, and the layout genuinely mirrors rather than being patched. This is rare and it is worth protecting.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] The result withholds the evidence

**What.** When an analysis returns, `MessageAnalyzer` unmounts and the text the student pasted is gone. `AnalysisResults` renders score → warning → clues → language tabs → explanation, and never shows the message. On top of that: the EN/AR `Tabs` is controlled by `value={locale === "ar" ? "ar" : "en"}` with no `onValueChange`, so both triggers are focusable, announce as tabs, and do nothing; the heading `results.whyFlagged` ("Why HARIS flagged this") renders for safe messages too; and nothing moves focus or scrolls when the result replaces the form.

**Why it matters.** The teaching method is "form your own hypothesis, then check it against the evidence". The result deletes the evidence at the exact moment of comparison, so the student is left matching three abstract sentences to a message they have to remember. Verdict-first plus message-absent turns a lesson into a pronouncement. The dead tabs are worse than missing: they make the bilingual promise look decorative, and a screen-reader user gets a tab that never changes.

**Fix.** Rebuild the result as an evidence workspace: keep the submitted message pinned at the top, in the language it was written, with the verdict beside it rather than above it. Make the language control actually switch (`onValueChange`), or drop the tabs and show both languages stacked. Move focus to the result heading and scroll to top on arrival. Condition the "flagged" heading on `is_threat` ("Why this looks safe" otherwise). Anchor each clue to the message — even a simple referential line ("clue 1: the 24-hour deadline") beats a detached paragraph.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable layout`

### [P1] False precision: a four-band judgement rendered as an instrument

**What.** The model returns `risk_band` ∈ {none, low, medium, high}. The server derives exactly four scores from it: 5, 15, 45, 85. The UI then draws a **28-segment gauge**, animates a percentage counting up, and labels the track `0 / 25 / 65 / 100` — with those labels spaced evenly by `justify-between` rather than positioned at 25% and 65%. The screenshot caught it mid-count at "84%", a number the system cannot produce.

**Why it matters.** This audience is explicitly chosen for "signal density" and "evidence", and PRODUCT.md's first principle is "instrument, not toy". A 28-segment meter over four possible values is the opposite of an instrument: it is costume. The thresholds printed on the track do not sit where the thresholds actually are. If a master's student notices that the gauge is decorative, they stop believing the verdict beside it — which is the one thing the app cannot afford.

**Fix.** Make the band the hero and stop implying resolution it does not have: a four-zone scale with the current zone marked and the boundaries labelled at their true positions, no percentage, no 28 ticks, no count-up. If a number is kept, show the band's representative score only after the animation settles, and never a value between bands. Keep `tabular-nums` and mono for the real data; that part is right.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable bolder` (to rebuild the verdict as a confident categorical readout) or `/impeccable distill`

### [P1] There is no trust contract at the point of paste

**What.** The home screen asks the student to paste "any suspicious message — SMS, WhatsApp, email, DM" into a 140px box. There is **no** privacy sentence, no statement about storage or retention, no note that the verdict is an AI judgement with limits, and no next step after a Dangerous verdict. `grep -i privacy src/locales/en.ts` returns nothing.

**Why it matters.** The primary action asks for potentially sensitive content — a real DM, a real email, sometimes someone else's words. Cautious users will not paste; careless users will overshare. And a security-awareness product that will not state its own limits is teaching the wrong lesson: "trust the tool" instead of "verify". "Dangerous. Do not click any links in this message" is a warning, not a recovery path.

**Fix.** One line under the textarea: what is processed, what is stored, for how long. One line near the score: this is an AI assessment, not a guarantee. Verdict-specific next steps: report it, block the sender, verify through the official app, keep the evidence. That turns a verdict into a lesson.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

### [P2] Colour means three different things

**What.** PRODUCT.md principle 5 reserves green/amber/red for safe/suspicious/dangerous. In practice: difficulty pills map Beginner→`success`, Intermediate→`warning`, Advanced→`destructive` (`src/lib/difficulty.ts`, whose own comment calls this "the one place in the app where green/amber/red is decorative rather than a verdict"); the clue cards use a blue/amber/red dot per clue number; Voice Lab's flag action is `destructive`. Red currently means "dangerous", "advanced", "clue 3", and "flag this".

**Why it matters.** Colour is the fastest signal in a risk tool. If red means four things, it means nothing at the moment it matters. The decorative use also makes status colour-only in places, which fails anyone who cannot separate the hues.

**Fix.** Reserve the green/amber/red ramp exclusively for risk. Use neutral or accent tokens for difficulty and actions, and number the clue dots in mono instead of colouring them. Pair every status colour with text or an icon.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable colorize`

### [P2] The product has not decided who it is for

**What.** PRODUCT.md: "Primary: cybersecurity master's students." About: "designed for high school students (K11–K12)." Scenarios: a free PS5, an Instagram giveaway, V-Bucks. Copy: "Train your instinct. Get ahead of the threats." The 404 says "Analyse a message"; the analyzer says "Analyze with HARIS".

**Why it matters.** This is not a wording issue. It sets vocabulary, stakes, and credibility. A master's student reads the product as outreach and discounts the verdict; a K11 student gets content pitched at them but a product that claims to be something else. No amount of visual refinement fixes a surface that is answering two briefs at once. It is also the root cause of the generic feel: a tool designed for everyone is designed for no one.

**Fix.** Settle it. If master's students are primary, the scenarios need stakes at that level (a recruiter, a fake MFA prompt, a supply-chain email) and the copy needs to stop explaining what phishing is. If both audiences are real, make them explicit tracks chosen at entry, with different scenario sets and different depth — not one averaged surface.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable shape`

## Persona Red Flags

**Sam — screen reader, keyboard only.** The brand is the only `h1` on five of six routes, so the document outline starts with "HARIS" rather than the page. There is no skip link. When an analysis finishes, focus stays on the submit button that no longer exists and nothing is announced, so Sam has to go hunting for the result. The language tabs are focusable, announce as a tablist, and never change content. The clue reveal button disappears and is replaced by a card without a live region, so the revealed clue may never be announced. On Sandbox, two `h1`s split the outline.

**Casey — phone, one hand, interrupted.** The sticky header is ~152px on a 390×844 viewport — 18% of the screen, before any content — and it holds nine things: brand, language, theme, level, XP bar, XP count, and five nav links. Nav links are ~34px tall and the language/theme controls are ~30px, under the 44px target. The "+10 XP" toast lands on top of the navigation on mobile. Interrupting the flow loses everything typed, and the Sandbox tab switch silently discards progress.

**Maha — the master's student the brief actually names, reading in Arabic.** She is told on About that this is for high schoolers, then handed V-Bucks. She reads "84%" on a 28-segment meter and correctly infers the number is invented, which undermines the verdict. She switches to Arabic and finds the result's language tabs frozen. In Arabic the XP bar still fills left-to-right inside an RTL layout, and directional chevrons point the wrong way. She concludes this is a school outreach site, not a tool for her level.

## Minor Observations

- "Analyse" (404) vs "Analyze" (everywhere else).
- "Live through a real attack" and "Hear a real scam call" — these are simulations; the copy overclaims at the exact place trust is built.
- Voice Lab prints "6 red flags" and "7 red flags" on the card before the call, which spoils the exercise it is about to set.
- Sandbox prints the same safety note twice (top and bottom), and exposes a "Spec & Dataset" tab in a learner-facing product.
- The Level 2 and Level 3 buttons on Sandbox overflow their own bounds at desktop width — "Unauthorized Tool Execution" runs into Level 3, and "RAG Data Exfiltration" is clipped. shadcn's `whitespace-nowrap` plus `flex-col` plus a 4-column grid is the cause.
- The three-step strip under the analyzer ("Paste the message / HARIS scores the threat / Discover it through clues") looks like a progress stepper and tracks nothing.
- A safe result is still headed "Why HARIS flagged this".
- The `+10 XP` toast overlaps the nav on mobile.
- The `risk.scale` gauge is announced to screen readers as a single image, so the score and the level are the only announced facts.
- Single path through everything: no way to reopen a past analysis, no history, no keyboard accelerators beyond tab order.

## Questions to Consider

1. If the result removes the message, what exactly is the student learning from — the verdict, or the evidence?
2. Why does the wait state have more craft and point of view than the result it waits for?
3. Who is this for: a master's seminar or a teenager on PS5? The answer changes every screen, and the product currently answers both.
4. If green/amber/red is a promise, why does red mean "dangerous", "advanced", "clue 3", and "flag this"?
5. Would you paste a real suspicious DM into this product? Nothing on the screen says you should.
6. What would the verdict look like if it were confident enough to have only four possible answers?
